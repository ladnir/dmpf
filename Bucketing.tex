\section{Bucketing DMPF}\label{sec:bucketing}

Bucketing is useful when the input addresses are independent and uniform.
Choose a public bucket count $k\mid N$, bucket capacity $c$, and
$M:=N/k$. Address $A_i$ belongs to bucket $\lfloor A_i/M\rfloor$ and has
local address $A_i\bmod M$. A padded Group-By operation collects the records
for each bucket. One DMPF per bucket produces the corresponding interval of
the output map.

If any bucket receives more than $c$ rows, the construction may omit inputs
and leak information through the Group-By opening.
For independent uniform addresses and $c<t$, a union bound gives
\[
 \Pr[\text{overflow}]
 \leq k\Pr[\operatorname{Bin}(t,1/k)>c]
 \leq k\binom{t}{c+1}k^{-(c+1)}.
\]
The probability is zero for $c\geq t$.
Choose $k,c$ to make this bound at most the desired $2^{-\lambda}$.
The choice $k\asymp t/\log t$ is a scaling heuristic, not by itself a
concrete security bound.

Let $C_{\mathsf{setup}}(M,c)$ and $C_{\mathsf{expand}}(M,c)$ be the costs
of the chosen DMPF. The total costs include $k$ such calls, the Group-By
cost, and $\Theta(N)$ output materialization.
They are not $O(t)$ independently of $N$.
The box below shows a single payload evaluation; it uses the reusable DMPF
interface but does not specify a separate reusable Group-By implementation.

\subsection{Group-By Operation}

For keys $A\in[0,N)^t$, payload records $B\in\mathbb G^t$, and capacity $p$,
Group-By returns $N$ groups of exactly $p$ records.
When every key occurs at most $p$ times, group $i$ contains its input records
and enough zero records to fill the group, in hidden random order.

Let $f_i$ be the count of key $i$ as an integer in $[0,t]$.
The count modulus is $2^\ell$ for $\ell:=\lceil\log_2(t+1)\rceil$;
in particular, it represents the allowed boundary count $f_i=p$ without
wrapping. For each key, create $p$ auxiliary records, of which
$p-\min(f_i,p)$ have label $i$ and selection bit one.
The others have label zero and selection bit zero.
Append the real records, all with selection bit one, and shuffle.
Opening the labels and selection bits identifies the groups, but not which
selected records are real: selected padding records also have bit one.

\begin{figure}[H]\small
\centering
\fbox{\begin{minipage}{.95\textwidth}
Public parameters are $N,t,p\geq1$ and an additive record group $\mathbb G$.
Put $\ell:=\lceil\log_2(t+1)\rceil$.\\
$\mathsf{Group\text{-}By}_{p,t,N}
 (\share A\in[0,N)^t,\share B\in\mathbb G^t)
 \longrightarrow\share G\in(\mathbb G^p)^N$:\\[0.3em]
The parties compute:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item $\share f:=\sum_{j\in[0,t)}\unitVec_{[0,N)}(\share A_j,1)
       \in\mathbb Z_{2^\ell}^{[0,N)}$.
 \item For $i\in[0,N)$, set
 $\share{d_i}:=(0)^{\min(\share f_i,p)}\Vert
               (1)^{p-\min(\share f_i,p)}$.
 \item $\share A':=0\cdot\share d_0\Vert\cdots\Vert
                  (N-1)\cdot\share d_{N-1}\Vert\share A$.
 \item $\share B':=0^{Np}\Vert\share B$.
 \item $\share d':=\share d_0\Vert\cdots\Vert\share d_{N-1}\Vert1^t$.
 \item $(\share A',\share B',\share d'):=
       \shuffle(\share A'\zip\share B'\zip\share d')$.
 \item $(A',d'):=\mathsf{reveal}(\share A'\zip\share d')$.
 \item For $i\in[0,N)$, let $\share G_i$ be the first $p$ records
 $\share B'_j$ with $A'_j=i$ and $d'_j=1$.
 \item Return $\share G$.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Correctness and opening distribution.}
If $f_i\leq p$ for every key, each group has $f_i$ input records and
$p-f_i$ selected zero records.
Exactly $t$ auxiliary records are unselected.
The opened pairs are therefore a uniform permutation of a fixed multiset:
$p$ copies of $(i,1)$ for each $i\in[0,N)$, and $t$ copies of $(0,0)$.
This describes the joint opening, not independent distributions for its
two fields. In the ideal-shuffle hybrid, it hides the group sizes on the
no-overflow event. Fresh output shares hide the retained payload records.
On overflow, a group may have more than $p$ selected records; truncation
keeps the output shape fixed, and the entire failed transcript is charged to
the caller's overflow bound.

Frequency evaluation and construction of the auxiliary records require
secret-index circuits. The explicit arrays already contain $Np+t$ records;
a dense frequency implementation processes $Nt$ indicator entries.
These costs must be included before comparing bucketing with another DMPF.
