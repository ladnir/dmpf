\subsection{Ring-LPN overview and its use in PCGs}\label{subsec:ringlpn}

Let $\mathbb R:=\mathbb F_q[X]/(X^{N_{\mathsf R}}+1)$.
For the NTT-based application, assume $2N_{\mathsf R}\mid(q-1)$.
The negacyclic NTT then maps ring multiplication to coordinate-wise
multiplication over $\mathbb F_q$.
For a specified sparse-noise distribution $\mathcal D$ on $\mathbb R$,
decisional Ring-LPN assumes
\[
 (r,re+f)\approx_c(r,u),
 \qquad r,u\gets\mathbb R,\quad e,f\gets\mathcal D.
\]
The samples on the right are independent. The noise distribution and
parameter family are part of the assumption \cite{boyle2020efficient}.

\paragraph{From sparse products to OLEs.}
Party $p\in\{0,1\}$ samples $e_{p,0},e_{p,1}\gets\mathcal D$.
The parties sample a public $r\gets\mathbb R$ and define
$x'_p:=e_{p,0}+re_{p,1}$. They need shares of
\begin{align*}
 y'&:=x'_0x'_1\\
   &=e_{0,0}e_{1,0}
     +r(e_{0,0}e_{1,1}+e_{0,1}e_{1,0})
     +r^2e_{0,1}e_{1,1}.
\end{align*}
Given those shares, local NTTs give $x_p:=\mathsf{NTT}(x'_p)$ and
$\share y:=\mathsf{NTT}(\share{y'})$ satisfying
$x_0\odot x_1=\share y_0+\share y_1$.
This is the OLE correlation used by the PCG. Two independent OLE
correlations yield one Beaver triple by local conversion.

Write $A_{p,i}\in[0,N_{\mathsf R})^t$ and
$B_{p,i}\in(\mathbb F_q\setminus\{0\})^t$ for a sparse polynomial's
positions and coefficients. For equally long lists $A,B$, define
$\mathsf{makePoly}(A,B):=\sum_j B_jX^{A_j}$.
Cartesian sums of positions and products of coefficients give
\[
 A'_{i,j}:=A_{0,i}\boxplus A_{1,j},\qquad
 B'_{i,j}:=B_{0,i}\otimes B_{1,j}.
\]
Both are lists of length $t^2$; repeated positions contribute their
coefficient sum. Their polynomial reduces to $e_{0,i}e_{1,j}$ modulo
$X^{N_{\mathsf R}}+1$.
A DMPF over $[0,2N_{\mathsf R})$ directly constructs its shared coefficient
map. Public ring operations then form the weighted sum above.

\paragraph{More than two noise polynomials.}
For a public polynomial count $P\geq2$, put $r_0:=1$ and sample
$r_i\gets\mathbb R$ for $i\in[1,P)$. Both parties use this same mask vector.
The two-polynomial example generalizes to
\[
 x'_p:=\sum_{i\in[0,P)}r_i e_{p,i},
 \qquad
 y':=\sum_{i,j\in[0,P)}r_i r_j e_{0,i}e_{1,j}.
\]
All $P$ noise polynomials enter one ring sample per party. They are not
split into independent two-polynomial instances.

\paragraph{Regular product lists.}
For regular noise, let $t\mid N_{\mathsf R}$ and
$L:=N_{\mathsf R}/t$.
Each party holds $P$ sparse polynomials, with one nonzero coefficient
in each of $t$ blocks. For $p\in\{0,1\}$, $a\in[0,P)$, and $j\in[0,t)$,
sample an offset $X_{p,a,j}\gets[0,L)$.
Its absolute ring position is $jL+X_{p,a,j}$.
For a polynomial pair $(a,b)$ and $k\in[0,t)$, define the ordered list
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ringlpn-product-list}
 A_{a,b,k}:=
 \bigl(X_{0,a,j}+X_{1,b,(k-j)\bmod t}\bigr)_{j\in[0,t)}
 \in[0,2L)^t.
\end{equation}
For row $j$, multiply the two coefficients and the sign
\[
 (-1)^{\lfloor(j+((k-j)\bmod t))/t\rfloor}.
\]
This sign accounts for negacyclic wraparound of the block positions.
After DMPF expansion, multiplication by $X^{kL}$ and reduction modulo
$X^{N_{\mathsf R}}+1$ also handle offsets that cross a block boundary.
Summing over $k$ gives the polynomial product.

Thus all $P^2$ cross-party polynomial products use $P^2t$ DMPF instances,
each with at most $t$ real points and domain length $2L$.
Regularity reorganizes the product terms; it does not reduce their number.
Our implementation uses $(P,t)=(4,16)$.
For a $wN$ DMPF expansion without a stash, the total leaf work is
$2wP^2N_{\mathsf R}$, rather than the
$2P^2tN_{\mathsf R}$ work of a sum of $t$ DPFs on each product list.

\subsection{Stationary LPN and repeated expansion}\label{sec:stationary}

Stationary noise retains the supports and refreshes their coefficients.
The stationary-decoding literature motivates this reuse \cite{Stationary};
the ring application requires a joint decisional assumption for the samples
it actually exposes.

Fix a polynomial expansion bound $Q(\kappa)$.
Sample the supports once from the prescribed distribution.
At expansion $u\in[0,Q)$, sample independent nonzero coefficients to form
$e^{(u)}_{p,i}$. Put $r_0^{(u)}:=1$ and independently sample
$r_i^{(u)}\gets\mathbb R$ for $i\in[1,P)$.
Write $\mathbf r^{(u)}:=(r_i^{(u)})_{i\in[0,P)}$.
For an honest party, the required statement is
\[
 \left(\mathbf r^{(u)},\sum_{i\in[0,P)}r_i^{(u)}e^{(u)}_{p,i}\right)_{u\in[0,Q)}
 \approx_c
 \bigl(\mathbf r^{(u)},U^{(u)}\bigr)_{u\in[0,Q)},
 \qquad U^{(u)}\gets\mathbb R.
\]
The replacements are independent across expansions and of the masks.
Marginal pseudorandomness for each expansion is not sufficient.
For multiple OLE instances, the assumption concerns their full joint
sequence as well. The Reverse-Cuckoo application additionally exposes
descriptors, as formalized in \namedref{Assumption}{ass:rc-decisional-lpn}.

The product-address lists $A'_{i,j}$ remain fixed.
Setup initializes their reusable DMPFs.
Each expansion forms new coefficient products $B'^{(u)}_{i,j}$, expands the
cached DMPFs, and computes
\[
 y'^{(u)}:=
 \sum_{i,j\in[0,P)}r_i^{(u)}r_j^{(u)}
 \mathsf{makePoly}(A'_{i,j},B'^{(u)}_{i,j})
 \pmod{X^{N_{\mathsf R}}+1}.
\]
The NTT yields the new OLE batch.
The same public mask vector is used for both parties within an expansion.
It is freshly sampled across expansions in this model.

\paragraph{Sampling and applying the masks.}
The NTT is a bijection. Sampling independent field elements in its output
coordinates therefore samples a uniform ring mask without an additional NTT.
At each expansion, the implementation regenerates those coordinates and
their pairwise products, retaining the allocated storage.
For $P$ masks with $r_0=1$, the current routine samples $(P-1)N_{\mathsf R}$
field elements and computes $P^2N_{\mathsf R}$ coordinate products per party.
This work does not change the supports or repeat the DMPF setup.

Reuse removes repeated address-dependent setup, not the linear leaf work.
Each expansion still converts the cached leaf seeds, exchanges payload
corrections, and performs ring arithmetic.
The generic DMPF argument and the stationary Ring-LPN assumption are separate:
the former hides arbitrary valid input indices through its ideal interface;
the latter justifies the pseudorandomness of the resulting correlation
sequence.
