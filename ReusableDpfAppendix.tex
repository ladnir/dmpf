\section{Reusable DPF Simulation}
\label{app:reusable-dpf-simulation}

We first simulate two random-value hybrids of
\namedref{Figures}{fig:Dpf} and~\ref{fig:sparseDpf}. We then reduce the real
protocol to these hybrids under explicit tree-generator and leaf-PRF
requirements. The reductions concern the abstract generators in the figures;
they do not establish those requirements for a particular AES evaluator.
Fix a static semi-honest corrupt party $P_b$, $b\in\{0,1\}$, and put
$h:=1-b$. Shared selections, products, and digit decomposition are ideal
circuit calls. Their fresh secret-output shares are uniform; the simulator
computes local operations and repeated uses of those shares consistently.

\subsection{Setup with independent honest active children}

Define the \emph{independent-child experiment} by changing only the honest
party's expansion of an active parent: replace that invocation of
$\mathsf G$ by $w$ fresh independent $\kappa$-bit children. Root-child shares
are already sampled directly. Keep every corrupt-party evaluation and every
off-path expansion unchanged.
The active node and the replacements are defined using the hidden index
inside the experiment; the simulator is not given that index.

\begin{lemma}[Correction-word distribution]
\label{lem:dpf-correction-distribution}
In the independent-child experiment, conditioned on the corrupt view before
each correction, the public correction has the following law, independent
of the secret child number:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item For binary branching, sample $a\gets\{0,1\}^{\kappa-1}$ and
 $\tau_1,\tau_2\gets\{0,1\}$ independently, and return
 $(a\Vert\tau_1,a\Vert\tau_2)$.
 \item For $w>2$, return $w$ independent $\kappa$-bit blocks.
\end{enumerate}
At an active split, the corrected honest active seed has a uniform
$(\kappa-1)$-bit prefix conditioned on the corrupt view after the correction
and on the secret child number.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix the corrupt pre-correction view and the secret digit $u\in[0,w)$.
At an active split, cancellation of off-path parents makes the reconstructed
child sums $z_1,\ldots,z_w$ the XORs of the active parent's two sets of
children. The honest children are independent uniform blocks, so these sums
are independent uniform blocks even after fixing the corrupt children.

For $w=2$, \figureref{fig:correction} reveals
\[
 a=\msbs(z_{2-u}),\qquad
 \tau_1=\lsb(z_1)\oplus u\oplus1,\qquad
 \tau_2=\lsb(z_2)\oplus u.
\]
These are independent uniform values. The prefix of $z_{1+u}$ is unused
by this disclosure. The corrected honest active prefix is that unused
prefix XOR a value fixed by the corrupt children, their parent tag, and
$a$. It therefore remains uniform after the disclosure. The active tag is
determined by correctness and is not claimed to be hidden.

For $w>2$, let $r\gets\{0,1\}^{\kappa-1}$ be the fresh reconstructed mask.
The correction is
\[
 \sigma_j=z_j\oplus[j=1+u](r\Vert1),\qquad j\in[1,w].
\]
For every fixed $r$, this is a bijection on the uniform tuple of child
sums. Thus the correction tuple is uniform and independent of $r$.
Conditioned on that tuple, the corrected honest active prefix is $r$ XOR
the corrected corrupt active prefix, so it is uniform.

At a sparse binary level with no active split, the reconstructed child sums
are zero. \figureref{fig:sparseDpf} replaces them by the independent uniform
mask $r$ before computing the correction. The binary correction therefore
has the same law at such a level. No active seed is changed there. Ideal
circuit-output shares add only independent local shares; deterministic
dependencies between local correction components are retained.
\end{proof}

The setup simulator now follows the public schedule. It samples the corrupt
root shares and the fresh output shares of ideal circuit calls. At each
visited split it samples the public correction according to
\namedref{Lemma}{lem:dpf-correction-distribution}. It computes the corrupt party's
local correction shares from its simulated state, and supplies their XOR
complement as the honest opening message. It then performs the corrupt
party's actual local corrections and generator evaluations. For sparse
trees, the visited split bits depend only on $S$; the correction law does
not reveal whether the active path splits at a visited bit.

Induction over this schedule gives the exact corrupt setup-view distribution
in the independent-child experiment, for either corruption choice. The
simulator saves its computed leaf seeds and counter. It does not need the
honest index to select a path or to construct those local seeds.

\subsection{Expansion with a fresh honest active value}

Fix a setup state satisfying the leaf invariant. For $x\in S$, write
$t_p(x):=\lsb(\share{s_x}_p)$ and let $u_p(x)\in\mathbb G$ denote the signed
leaf conversion of party $p$. Off the active address $\alpha$, the two tags
agree and $u_0(x)+u_1(x)=0$.

Define the \emph{fresh-active-value experiment} by replacing only the honest
conversion $u_h(\alpha)$ in each expansion with a fresh uniform element of
$\mathbb G$, independent of the history and current inputs. All corrupt
conversions are still computed from the saved seeds with the public
$\mathsf L_q$. Honest off-path conversions remain their negatives. The
replacement is a distributional hybrid; it is not an extra protocol step.

\begin{lemma}[Online leaf-update simulation]
\label{lem:dpf-leaf-update-simulation}
In the fresh-active-value experiment, the expansion messages and corrupt
output can be simulated exactly from the corrupt local state and payload
share. The honest output equals the complement prescribed by
$\mathcal F_{\mathsf{rDPF}}$. The same simulator works online for
polynomially many adaptive expansions without changing the saved seeds.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $\eta:=t_0(\alpha)-t_1(\alpha)\in\{-1,1\}$, where the tags are
interpreted as integers. The correctness calculation gives the opened value
\[
 \gamma=\eta\bigl(\beta-u_0(\alpha)-u_1(\alpha)\bigr).
\]
Fix the preceding history, the current inputs, and the corrupt conversions.
The remaining honest active value is uniform. Translation and multiplication
by either sign are bijections of $\mathbb G$, so $\gamma$ is uniform and
independent of that fixed information.

The simulator computes all $u_b(x)$ and $t_b(x)$ from its saved state and
samples $\gamma\gets\mathbb G$. In characteristic two, the corrupt opening
share is the locally computed
$\gamma_b:=\share\beta_b-\sum_{x\in S}u_b(x)$.
In other characteristics, the simulator computes the corrupt sign-bit share
from its tags, and samples its fresh output share $\gamma_b$ from the ideal
sign-multiplication call. In either case, it supplies
$\gamma-\gamma_b$ as the honest opening message. These are the exact
opening-share distributions in the stated experiment.

It computes and submits the map
\[
 y_b(x):=u_b(x)+(1-2b)\gamma t_b(x),\qquad x\in S,
\]
to $\mathcal F_{\mathsf{rDPF}}$. Off $\alpha$, the two corrected values
cancel. At $\alpha$, their sum is
$u_0(\alpha)+u_1(\alpha)+\eta\gamma=\beta$.
The honest map is consequently $\mathsf{pt}_S(\alpha,\beta)-y_b$,
exactly as returned by the functionality. This proves the joint-output
claim. At the next call the simulator retains the same seeds, increments
$q$, and uses fresh simulation randomness. The fresh honest active value
has the same conditional law for every ensuing choice of payload; induction
proves the adaptive claim. A singleton uses the ideal identity circuit
instead: sample its corrupt output share and submit it to the functionality.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Generator requirements and reductions}

Let $H_0$ be the real generator-based protocol with ideal shared-circuit
calls. Let $H_1$ make the independent-child replacements during setup,
and let $H_2$ additionally make the fresh-active-value replacements during
expansion. The lemmas above give an exact online simulator for $H_2$.
We justify the transitions using the following two primitive requirements.

For the tree generator, define
$\mathsf G_\theta(k):=\mathsf G(k\Vert\theta)$ for
$k\in\{0,1\}^{\kappa-1}$ and $\theta\in\{0,1\}$.
The challenge algorithm chooses $\theta$ before receiving either
$\mathsf G_\theta(K)$ for $K\gets\{0,1\}^{\kappa-1}$, or $w$ independent
uniform $\kappa$-bit blocks. It does not receive $K$.
Let $\epsilon_{\mathsf G}$ bound its distinguishing advantage.
Its preceding state is independent of $K$, and it may evaluate the public
generators on inputs of its choice. This requirement includes knowledge of
the tag; ordinary PRG security for an unrestricted seed does not imply it.

For leaf conversion, use the PRF option
$\mathsf L_q(k):=\mathsf L(q,k)$ from \sectionref{sec:dpf}.
Let $\epsilon_{\mathsf L}(Q)$ bound the advantage of distinguishing
oracle access to $\mathsf L(\cdot,K)$ from a random function into
$\mathbb G$, for a uniform secret key $K$ and at most $Q$ queries.
The algorithm may also evaluate the public family under chosen keys.
This is one joint requirement across rounds, not a separate assumption
for each $q$. Both primitive bounds below are evaluated at the runtime of
the external distinguisher plus one execution of the protocol and its
ideal circuits.

\begin{theorem}[Reusable DPF simulation]
\label{thm:reusable-dpf-simulation}
Fix valid public parameters and a nonempty support $S$. Let $a_S$ bound
the number of active-parent calls to $\mathsf G$ after the directly sampled
root children, maximized over $\alpha\in S$.
Under the two primitive requirements above, the standard and sparse DPF
protocols realize $\mathcal F_{\mathsf{rDPF}}$ against static semi-honest
corruption for $Q$ polynomially many adaptive expansions, with advantage
\[
 \epsilon_{\mathsf{DPF}}(Q)
 \leq a_S\epsilon_{\mathsf G}+\epsilon_{\mathsf L}(Q)
       +\epsilon_{\mathsf{circuit}}(Q),
\]
where $\epsilon_{\mathsf{circuit}}(Q)$ is the accumulated joint simulation
loss of the shared-circuit realizations. For a nontrivial standard tree,
$a_S\leq D-1$; for a sparse binary tree,
$a_S\leq\min\{D-1,|S|-2\}$. A singleton needs only the ideal identity
circuit and incurs neither generator term.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
First replace active-parent expansions in root-to-leaf order. Consider
two adjacent experiments, differing only at the next active parent.
All earlier active expansions have independent children.
\namedref{Lemma}{lem:dpf-correction-distribution} makes this parent's honest
prefix a uniform secret $K$, independent of the preceding corrupt view.
Its tag $\theta$ is fixed by the corrupt tag and the active path.

The reduction runs the external distinguisher and the surrounding execution,
including the honest inputs. Unlike the final simulator, it therefore
knows the active path. It leaves $K$ implicit in the honest parent state,
chooses $\theta$, and uses its challenge as that parent's children.
This embedding does not require $K$: at the preceding correction, the
honest active prefix was uniform and unexposed. In the binary case its
prefix was unused by the correction; in the wider case the fresh mask
made it independent of the correction. Local ideal-output shares can be
sampled without exposing that prefix.
The parent is used only for this expansion. All off-path seeds are
computable from the corrupt state, and all subsequent children are
computable from the challenge. Earlier honest parents are not needed again.
If the path has no such call, the adjacent experiments coincide.

The reduction follows all later corrections and expansions, and returns
the external distinguisher's bit. It obtains honest output maps from
correctness and the computed corrupt maps, preserving the joint external
execution. A real challenge gives the earlier experiment; a uniform
challenge gives the later experiment. There are at most $a_S$ transitions,
each using one challenge, so their total loss is at most
$a_S\epsilon_{\mathsf G}$.

In $H_1$, the final honest active prefix $K$ is uniform and independent
of the corrupt setup view. For $H_1$ to $H_2$, a second reduction leaves
that prefix implicit and uses its PRF oracle for every honest active
leaf conversion. It generates the setup view by the exact setup simulation
above. It computes all corrupt conversions locally; honest off-path
conversions are their negatives. On call $q$, it queries its oracle at
$q$, applies the honest party's sign, and performs the leaf update.
The hidden prefix has no other post-setup use. The reduction can generate
the opening messages and both output maps without knowing that prefix.

A real PRF oracle gives $H_1$. A random-function oracle gives $H_2$,
because the stored counter never repeats an input. The reduction makes
at most $Q$ queries and returns the external distinguisher's bit.
It receives payloads as the execution proceeds, so their dependence on
earlier honest outputs does not change the reduction. This transition
costs $\epsilon_{\mathsf L}(Q)$, with no additional factor of $Q$.
Neither reduction rewinds or aborts. The exact simulator for $H_2$ and
composition with the shared-circuit simulators give the stated bound.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Concrete AES evaluators}
\label{app:dpf-aes}

The theorem does not require fresh public randomness during expansion.
A concrete AES instantiation must justify its tree evaluator, stored-seed
representation, and leaf evaluator, accounting for forward and inverse
queries, collisions, and domain separation.

\paragraph{Existing libOTe evaluator.}
For dense expansion, we retain an AES-based evaluator from earlier
\texttt{libOTe} code~\cite{libOTe}. This is an inherited implementation
choice, not a new tree-generator design. With public AES key $k$ and seed
$s\in\{0,1\}^{128}$, it computes
\[
 t:=\mathsf{AES}_k(s),\qquad
 \bigl(\mathsf{AESRound}(t,s),\mathsf{add64x2}(t,s)\bigr).
\]
Here $\mathsf{AESRound}$ is one AES round, including its round-key XOR;
$\mathsf{add64x2}$ adds the two 64-bit lanes separately modulo $2^{64}$.
We conjecture that this optimization preserves DPF privacy. Related work on
expand-accumulate codes conjectures punctured-function unpredictability for
$(P(s)\oplus s,P(s)+s\bmod 2^\kappa)$ with ideal permutation
$P$~\cite[Section~6.2, Conjecture~6.4]{pcgExpandAccumulate}.
The evaluator's additional AES round is intended to disrupt that candidate's
direct bit and carry relations. Neither ideal AES nor the cited analysis
proves this evaluator secure or instantiates the theorem above.

For sparse binary expansion, define
$H_k(s):=\mathsf{AES}_k(s)\oplus s$ for $s\in\{0,1\}^{128}$.
The children are $(H_k(s),H_k(s\oplus1))$, where $1$ is the block whose
only set bit is the low bit. The dense kernel exports its corrected seed
$s$ through $\mathsf{AESRound}(s,s)$ before the sparse interface.
The reusable sparse interface stores a 127-bit seed prefix and a separate
tag; leaf conversion pads the prefix with a zero low bit.
These representation steps are also part of the concrete instantiation.
The rekeying schedules below retain the existing child-generation formulas.

\paragraph{Internal-node rekeying.}
Setup derives purpose-separated public 128-bit roots from its agreed public
coins. Dense and sparse expansion use separate roots, as do Waterfall's
column and value-scatter DPFs. Fix one such root $R$.
Write $\langle c,j\rangle$ for a fixed injective 128-bit encoding of a
64-bit purpose label $c$ and a 64-bit counter $j$.
The labels $\mathsf{node}$, $\mathsf{leaf}$, and $\mathsf{next}$ below are
distinct. Internal counter position $j$ uses
\[
 k_j:=\mathsf{AES}_R\bigl(\langle\mathsf{node},\lfloor j/1024\rfloor\rangle\bigr).
\]
One parent producing two children consumes one position. Sparse traversal
starts at zero and continues across trees and levels, including
dense-to-sparse boundary expansions. Dense recomputation restores each
node's position in public per-tree, per-level counter ranges. Thus revisiting
a node preserves its key. Each chunk contains at most 1,024 distinct parent
positions; neither counter assignment depends on the secret active path.

\paragraph{Cached-leaf rekeying.}
Each cached-leaf stream has a separate public root $R_0$. Number its leaves
$j=0,1,\ldots$ in public row order, continuing across row boundaries.
Expansion $q$ derives its keys and advances its root by
\[
 k_{q,j}:=\mathsf{AES}_{R_q}
     \bigl(\langle\mathsf{leaf},\lfloor j/1024\rfloor\rangle\bigr),
 \qquad
 R_{q+1}:=\mathsf{AES}_{R_q}(\langle\mathsf{next},0\rangle).
\]
For a saved prefix $s\in\{0,1\}^{127}$, conversion applies
$H_{k_{q,j}}(s\Vert0)$ before coefficient conversion and the party's sign.
For $\mathbb G=\mathbb Z_{2^{64}}$, coefficient conversion takes the low
64 bits. Other coefficient contexts use their specified conversion maps.
The extra public context $j$ is part of this concrete evaluator; the
figures use the abstract notation $\mathsf L_q$.
The roots advance deterministically, so rekeying adds no DMPF messages or
OTs and requires no fresh public coins during expansion. The implementation
reuses each expanded AES key across a chunk and retains eight-node batching.

Rekeying is intended to limit same-key collision effects. It does not
establish the generator requirements or remove collisions within a chunk. A concrete
proof must handle the public schedules jointly with setup openings, dense
export, and all leaf conversions. Distinct purpose encodings do not by
themselves prove independence across derived roots or expansion rounds.
