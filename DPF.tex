\section{Single-Point DPF Constructions}\label{sec:dpf}

Our applications reuse one secret index with many payloads.  We therefore
separate the index-dependent setup from each payload-dependent expansion.  This
separation also makes the persistent state and the permitted reuse explicit.

Fix $\kappa,N\in\mathbb N$, an ordered public set $S\subseteq[0,N)$, and a
finite additive group $\mathbb G$.
We write $\mathbb G^S$ for the set of maps from $S$ to $\mathbb G$.  For
$\alpha\in S$ and $\beta\in\mathbb G$, define the point map
\[
 \mathsf{pt}_{S}(\alpha,\beta)(x):=
 \begin{cases}
  \beta,&x=\alpha,\\
  0,&x\neq\alpha,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad x\in S.
\]

The reusable DPF functionality maintains one index and a public expansion
counter.  Setup receives shares of the index.  Expansion number $q$ receives
shares of one payload and produces an additive sharing of the corresponding
point map.  The corrupt party $P_b$, $b\in\{0,1\}$, chooses its output share;
the functionality supplies the honest party's complementary share.
Payloads may depend on the transcript of earlier expansions.

A reusable DPF realization has the same interface:
\begin{align*}
 \mathsf{DPF.Setup}_{S}(\share\alpha\in S)
   &\longrightarrow \mathsf{ready},\\
 \mathsf{DPF.Expand}_{S}(\share\beta\in\mathbb G)
   &\longrightarrow \share y\in\mathbb G^S.
\end{align*}
The protocol instance maintains internal state $(\share s,q)$, initially
undefined.  Party $p$ stores only $(\share s_p,q)$.  Setup initializes $q:=0$,
and each expansion increments it. Calls have valid inputs in their declared
domains; in particular, setup requires $\alpha\in S$. No membership-test
circuit is included. Repeated setup and expansion before setup return $\bot$.

Fix a polynomial $Q(\kappa)$.  A protocol realizes
$\mathcal F_{\mathsf{rDPF}}$ for $Q$ expansions if the following condition
holds.  For each $b\in\{0,1\}$ and every static semi-honest PPT adversary
corrupting $P_b$, there exists a stateful PPT simulator.  Given only public
parameters and $P_b$'s input shares, the simulator generates its view and
submits its output share $y_b$ to the functionality on each expansion.
The joint simulated view and honest outputs must be computationally
indistinguishable from their real counterparts for at most $Q(\kappa)$
adaptively chosen payloads.  The view includes persistent local state,
messages, and output shares.  The simulator proceeds online without
rewinding or receiving future inputs.

For a nontrivial tree, setup performs the index-dependent oblivious
transfers, expands the tree, and saves the leaf-seed shares.
Expansion number $q$ converts those seeds with $\mathsf L_q$ and applies
$\updateLeave$ to the new payload. Let $\epsilon_{\mathsf{DPF}}(Q)$ denote
the simulation advantage for all $Q$ adaptive expansions jointly.
\namedref{Theorem}{thm:reusable-dpf-simulation} bounds this advantage under
the tag-conditioned tree-PRG and joint leaf-PRF requirements stated in
\appendixref{app:reusable-dpf-simulation}. Correctness alone does not give
this guarantee, which Waterfall requires when replacing ideal DPF calls by
a concrete realization. The AES evaluators and rekeying schedules in
\appendixref{app:dpf-aes} remain separate instantiation claims.

For a singleton $S=\{x\}$, the point map is just the payload at $x$.
Setup stores the initialized flag and counter, without a tree.
Expansion invokes the ideal identity circuit $\mathsf{reshare}(\share\beta)$
and returns its result at $x$. Empty sets require no DPF instance.

\subsection{Standard DPF Protocol}
 

We use the tree-based DPF approach of Gilboa--Ishai \cite{dpf}
and its refinements \cite{dpf2,dpfKeygen}. A public branching factor $w\geq2$
gives a complete tree with $w^D$ leaves, where $D=\lceil\log_w n\rceil$.
Only the first $n$ leaves are returned. The input $\alpha\in[0,n)$ selects
one root-to-leaf path.

\paragraph{Intuition.}
After correction, the parties hold equal seed shares at every off-path node.
Those nodes expand identically and therefore cancel. The active node has
tag shares whose XOR is one. Before correction, both parties expand their
local seeds, producing $w$ children per node. They sum child seeds by child
number and compute one public correction per child number.
The parent tags gate these corrections: inactive parents preserve equal
child shares, while the active parent's children cancel except on the
selected path.

For binary branching, the two corrections share their upper $\kappa-1$
bits. Their low bits set the selected child's reconstructed tag to one and
the other tag to zero. For $w>2$, the correction procedure instead leaves
one fresh shared seed on the selected child. Child numbers are one-based;
the secret base-$w$ digit is zero-based.

\paragraph{Leaf update.}
Each party converts its saved seed prefix using $\mathsf L_q$ and uses
opposite signs in the output group. Off-path values cancel.
The parties open a single correction $\gamma$ and apply it using their
saved leaf tags. For groups of characteristic other than two,
$\updateLeave$ first corrects the sign induced by the XOR-shared tags.
Its shared selections and products are ideal circuit calls.

Fix a round-indexed family of leaf PRGs
\[
 (\mathsf L_q)_{q\geq0},
 \qquad
 \mathsf L_q:\{0,1\}^{\kappa-1}\rightarrow\mathbb G.
\]
The family may instead be instantiated by a domain-separated PRF
$\mathsf L:\mathbb N\times\{0,1\}^{\kappa-1}\rightarrow\mathbb G$,
where the second argument is the secret key and $q$ is the public input, with
$\mathsf L_q(s):=\mathsf L(q,s)$.  Thus the public expansion number $q$
selects the function applied to each saved leaf seed.

\paragraph{Standard-tree correctness.}
At the root, the reconstructed parent tag is one. At every subsequent
level, equal off-path seeds expand equally, so only the active parent's
children contribute to the reconstructed child sums $z$.
For binary branching, the correction cancels the off-path child and sets
the two reconstructed tags to $1-\alpha_d,\alpha_d$.
For larger branching, the corrected child sums are zero except at
child $1+\alpha_d$, whose reconstructed seed is $r\Vert1$.
Induction therefore gives equal off-path leaf seeds and one active leaf
with reconstructed tag one. Because $\alpha<n$, truncating the padded
tree to its first $n$ leaves preserves that leaf.
The sign calculation in the proof of \namedref{Proposition}{prop:sparse-leaf-invariant}
then shows that $\updateLeave$ reconstructs the requested point map for
each payload. This argument establishes correctness, not the reusable
simulation guarantee defined above.

\subsection{Sparse DPF}\label{sec:sparse}

The standard DPF expands a full tree, even when the public set
$S\subseteq[0,2^D)$ contains few indices. The sparse construction retains only
the leaves in $S$ and the nodes where their paths branch. A seed passes
unchanged through a chain with one live child. Each retained internal node
has two children, so setup expands only $|S|-1$ branching nodes.

We number bits from $D$ at the root to $1$ at the leaves.
The public queue $u_d$ contains nodes whose next split is at bit $d$;
$u_0$ contains leaves. A queue entry
$(j,\rho,b,\share s,\share t)$ records the child number
$j\in\{1,2\}$, its parent's split bit $\rho$, and its next child intervals
$b$. Its seed still requires correction $\sigma_{\rho,j}$, controlled by
the saved parent tag $\share t$.
For $u_0$, the sole address is $S_{b_{1,1}}$.
Correction depth $\rho$ and expansion depth $d$ are different: an edge can
skip several bits.

\paragraph{Correction scheduling.}
Let $d_*$ be the first split bit of $S$. Setup samples the two root-child
seed shares directly and records their sums in $z_{d_*}$.
Both children can be leaves, leaving every positive-depth queue empty.
The root correction must nevertheless be computed.

At every visited split bit $d$, setup first processes $u_d$.
A queued node consumes its parent's correction $\sigma_{\rho,j}$ before
expansion. Since $\rho>d$, that correction already exists.
The node then produces two children, whose uncorrected seeds contribute to
$z_d$. Their correction is indexed by this split bit $d$, not by $d-1$.
Each child waits in its next-split queue, or in $u_0$ if it is a leaf.
Only after all nodes in $u_d$ have expanded does setup compute $\sigma_d$.

The reconstructed bit $v_d$ indicates whether the active path splits at $d$.
It starts at zero, except for $v_{d_*}=1$, and accumulates the corrected
parent tags. If $v_d=0$, both reconstructed child sums vanish.
Setup masks those sums with fresh shared randomness before revealing a
correction. This preserves the inactive nodes, whose parent tags reconstruct
to zero. It also avoids revealing the absence of an active split through
deterministically zero sums.

For example, take $S=\{0,1,4\}$ with $D=3$.
The root splits at bit $3$; its left child waits in $u_1$ and its right child
waits in $u_0$. Setup computes $\sigma_3$, skips bit $2$, and then expands
$u_1$ using $\sigma_3$. The new children contribute to $z_1$ before
$\sigma_1$ is computed. At the end, leaves $0,1$ consume $\sigma_1$ and leaf
$4$ consumes $\sigma_3$.

\paragraph{Correctness.}
The next proposition concerns the reconstructed map, not the distribution
of either party's output share. The shared selections and products are
evaluated by their ideal circuit functionalities.

\begin{proposition}[Sparse leaf invariant]\label{prop:sparse-leaf-invariant}
Fix $S\subseteq[0,2^D)$ with $|S|>1$ and $\alpha\in S$.
For every choice of setup randomness, \figureref{fig:sparseDpf} saves seed
shares satisfying
\[
 \share{s_x}_0=\share{s_x}_1\quad(x\neq\alpha),
 \qquad
 \lsb(\share{s_x}_0)\oplus\lsb(\share{s_x}_1)=[x=\alpha].
\]
Consequently, every expansion reconstructs
$\mathsf{pt}_S(\alpha,\beta)$, for every payload $\beta\in\mathbb G$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Before any split, exactly one live subtree contains $\alpha$.
Inductively, every corrected inactive seed reconstructs to zero, whereas the
active seed has reconstructed tag one. Equal local seeds expand identically,
so inactive parents contribute zero to the reconstructed child sums.
If the active parent splits at $d$, the sums $z_{d,1},z_{d,2}$ therefore
equal its two child seeds.
The binary correction makes its off-path child zero and sets the tags
to $1-\alpha_d,\alpha_d$. All other children have reconstructed parent tag
zero and retain reconstructed seed zero.
If the active parent does not split, every processed parent is inactive;
the masked correction again preserves their reconstructed zero seeds.
Children can defer their correction because neither their seeds nor their
saved parent tags change while queued. This proves the invariant at every
split and, finally, at every leaf.

During expansion, equal off-path seeds receive equal values from
$\mathsf L_q$ with opposite signs. Thus the provisional reconstructed map
vanishes off $\alpha$. Write $t_{p,x}:=\lsb(\share{s_x}_p)$ and
$c_p:=\sum_{x\in S}t_{p,x}$ as integers.
The tag invariant gives
\[
 c_0-c_1=1-2t_{1,\alpha},
 \qquad
 \left(\left\lfloor c_0/2\right\rfloor+
       \left\lfloor c_1/2\right\rfloor+c_1\right)\bmod2
       =t_{1,\alpha}.
\]
Hence the bit $d$ in $\updateLeave$ equals $t_{1,\alpha}$.
If $\gamma_0:=\beta-\sum_x y_x$ denotes the needed correction,
the revealed value is $(1-2t_{1,\alpha})\gamma_0$.
Its reconstructed update at $\alpha$ is
$(1-2t_{1,\alpha})^2\gamma_0=\gamma_0$ and is zero elsewhere.
In characteristic two the sign conversion is unnecessary.
The argument holds for each payload without changing the saved seeds.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Cost and proof scope.}
The compressed binary tree has $|S|-1$ branching nodes.
Setup samples the root children directly and calls $\mathsf G$ once per
party at each of the remaining $|S|-2$ branching nodes.
It generates one correction pair per distinct split bit, at most $D$ pairs.
Expansion applies $\mathsf L_q$ once per saved leaf and then
$\updateLeave$. The scheduling correction adds neither tree expansions nor
correction pairs.

Each binary correction selection uses two OTs with block-length messages.
Masking a visited sparse level uses two further OTs with two-block messages;
the XOR of corrected parent tags supplies its secret control bit.
A dense prefix can avoid masks at its complete levels: expand that prefix
as a standard binary tree, then prune the remaining public subtrees.
The prefix's corrected tags initialize activity at the subsequent sparse
splits. For groups outside characteristic two, each instance also caches
the sign-conversion correlations used by $\updateLeave$.
These costs belong to initialization; they do not add leaf conversions to
repeated local expansion.

\namedref{Proposition}{prop:sparse-leaf-invariant} proves correctness for valid
inputs with $|S|>1$; singleton correctness and ideal-call simulation follow
from the identity-circuit interface. A simulation proof for
$\mathcal F_{\mathsf{rDPF}}$ must additionally reproduce the corrupt party's
view jointly with the honest output shares across expansions.
The leaf invariant alone does not establish that distributional claim.
\namedref{Theorem}{thm:reusable-dpf-simulation} proves the corresponding simulation
under its stated generator requirements, without prescribing the corrupt
output share.
