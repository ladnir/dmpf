\section{Reverse Cuckoo for Ring-LPN}
\label{sec:revCuckoo}

Waterfall Cuckoo is our generic DMPF construction.  This section gives a
smaller, application-specific alternative for the random sparse supports in
our Ring-LPN PCG.  Reverse Cuckoo reduces the dominant local expansion from
$3N$ or $4N$ to $2N$: instead of sampling hashes and then finding a placement,
it samples a hidden placement and programs public hashes around it.  The price
is support leakage. We first describe the $K=1$ construction, then derive
its output distribution in the ideal hash model. The concrete evaluator
requires a separate correctness analysis and leakage assumption.

Here $K$ counts independently generated placement-first proposals passed to an
optional public debiasing rule; $K=1$ simply publishes the sole proposal.  We
lead with $K=1$ because it needs no proposal generation or comparison beyond
the single published descriptor tuple. Larger $K$ adds setup work to reduce
the idealized leakage, but does not reduce the $2N$ expansion cost.
The attack analysis therefore starts with the unmitigated $K=1$ channel.

\subsection{Concrete feature evaluator}\label{sec:hash}

Reverse Cuckoo must program a public hash after the input rows have been
shuffled.  We use a degree-two feature evaluator whose only nonlinear layer is
one batch of bit multiplications.  Let
$n:=\lceil\log_2(N+1)\rceil$, let
$\mathsf{bin}_N:[0,N]\rightarrow\F_2^n$ be the canonical encoding, and write
$\nu:=\mathsf{bin}_N(N)$.  A seed expands to independent uniform matrices
\[
 A_0,A_1\in\F_2^{n\times q'},
 \qquad
 A_2\in\F_2^{(n+q')\times q}.
\]
For $x\in[0,N]$, define
\[
 a(x):=\mathsf{bin}_N(x)A_0,
 \qquad
 b(x):=\mathsf{bin}_N(x)A_1,
 \qquad
 c(x):=a(x)\odot b(x),
\]
where $\odot$ is coordinate-wise multiplication in $\F_2^{q'}$.  The
anchored feature vector and evaluator are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rc-concrete-feature}
 \Phi(x):=\bigl(\mathsf{bin}_N(x)\oplus\nu,\,
                  c(x)\oplus c(N)\bigr),
 \qquad
 F(x):=\Phi(x)A_2.
\end{equation}
Thus $F(N)=0$.  Each partition uses an independent seed, sampled jointly
after the shuffled rows are fixed.  Under the ideal-cipher model for the
seed expander, the three matrices are uniform.

The two matrix products defining $a$ and $b$, the anchoring, and the final
compression are linear over $\F_2$.  Evaluating one row therefore requires
only the $q'$ secure bit products in $a(x)\odot b(x)$.  The implementation
sets
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rc-hash-widths}
 q'=q=d+\lambda+8,
\end{equation}
up to byte alignment.  The fixed eight-bit margin is part of this evaluator;
it does not change the independently chosen statistical parameter $\lambda$.
The exact reason for the margin is given after the protocol.

\subsection{The \texorpdfstring{$K=1$}{K=1} construction}

Fix a domain $[0,N)$, a maximum support size $t$, two partitions of size $d$,
and $m:=2d$.  We take $d$ to be a power of two and write
$\ell:=\log_2d$.  Setup receives
$\share A\in\mathcal X_N^t$, and each expansion receives
$\share B\in\mathbb G^t$.  As elsewhere, the public symbol
$N\notin[0,N)$ denotes a dummy address and has payload zero.

For each partition $s\in[0,2)$, instantiate \equationref{eq:rc-concrete-feature}
with an independent seed, and denote the resulting evaluator by
$F_s:[0,N]\rightarrow\F_2^q$.  Let
$\mathsf{enc}_d:[0,d)\rightarrow\F_2^\ell$ be binary encoding and let
$\mathsf{dec}_d$ denote its inverse.  A public descriptor
$R_s\in\F_2^{q\times\ell}$ defines the hash
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rc-programmed-hash}
 h_s(x):=\mathsf{dec}_d\bigl(F_s(x)R_s\bigr)\in[0,d).
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Place first.}
Setup deduplicates the addresses, appends $m-t$ copies of $N$, and applies a
uniform secret shuffle.  Write $\bar A_r$ for row $r\in[0,m)$ after the shuffle.
The protocol retains the shuffle and applies it to each later deduplicated
payload vector.  Write $B'_r$ for the corresponding shuffled payload.  Row
$r=sd+b$, for $s\in[0,2)$ and $b\in[0,d)$, is interpreted as the physical bin
$(s,b)$.  Since the dummy rows are indistinguishable and the shuffle is
uniform, the real rows occupy a uniform injection into the $m$ bins.

\paragraph{Program one partition at a time.}
For partition $s$, form the secret matrix and public target
\[
 M_s:=
 \begin{bmatrix}
  F_s(\bar A_{sd})\\[-1mm]
  \vdots\\[-1mm]
  F_s(\bar A_{sd+d-1})
 \end{bmatrix}
 \in\F_2^{d\times q},
 \qquad
 Y_d:=
 \begin{bmatrix}
  \mathsf{enc}_d(0)\\[-1mm]
  \vdots\\[-1mm]
  \mathsf{enc}_d(d-1)
 \end{bmatrix}
 \in\F_2^{d\times\ell}.
\]
Invoke $\ell$ parallel instances of \textsf{bin-solver} to sample
$\share R_s$ satisfying the independent rows of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rc-programming-system}
 M_sR_s=Y_d.
\end{equation}
The solver natively skips a row that reduces to zero, regardless of its
right-hand side.  Hence a dummy row, for which $F_s(N)=0$, imposes no
constraint.  Reveal $R_0,R_1$ after both systems have been solved.

If a real record $x=\bar A_{sd+b}$ occupies row $sd+b$, then its equation is
$F_s(x)R_s=\mathsf{enc}_d(b)$ and therefore $h_s(x)=b$.  Thus its shuffled row is a
valid cuckoo position under the revealed hashes.

\paragraph{Build the sparse columns.}
The public position map $P\in\{0,1\}^{[0,N)\times m}$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rc-position-map}
 P_{x,sd+h_s(x)}=1
 \quad\text{for every }x\in[0,N),\ s\in[0,2),
\end{equation}
with all other entries zero.  Column $j\in[0,m)$ has sparse domain
$S_j:=\{x\in[0,N):P_{x,j}=1\}$.  Setup replaces each dummy in column $j$ by a
public representative of $S_j$ and initializes one reusable sparse DPF over
$S_j$.  The corresponding payload is always zero.  Expanding all columns and
adding them at their original addresses yields
$\sum_{i\in[0,t)}\unitVec_{[0,N)}(A_i,B_i)$.

\subsection{Correctness of the programmed systems}
\label{sec:revCuckooHashCorrectness}

Fresh matrices do not make the evaluator rows independent.  Its degree-two
structure instead permits an exact relation-by-relation analysis.  Fix the
real points $x_1,\ldots,x_r$ assigned to one partition.  Stack their evaluator
outputs and public row labels in
\[
 H_A:=\begin{bmatrix}F(x_1)\\[-1mm]\vdots\\[-1mm]F(x_r)\end{bmatrix}
 \in\F_2^{r\times q},
 \qquad
 Y\in\F_2^{r\times\ell}.
\]
A nonzero $\alpha\in\F_2^r$ is harmful if
$\alpha^\top H_A=0$ but $\alpha^\top Y\neq0$.

\begin{lemma}[Survival of a fixed relation]\label{lem:rc-relation-survival}
Write $\bar x_i:=\mathsf{bin}_N(x_i)$ and define
\[
 L_\alpha:=\bigoplus_i\alpha_i(\bar x_i\oplus\nu),
 \qquad
 Q_\alpha:=\bigoplus_i\alpha_i\bar x_i^\top\bar x_i
       \oplus\left(\bigoplus_i\alpha_i\right)\nu^\top\nu.
\]
If $L_\alpha\neq0$, then the output relation survives with probability
$2^{-q}$.  If $L_\alpha=0$, then $Q_\alpha$ is alternating and has even rank
$\rho$.  In this case the survival probability is exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rc-relation-survival}
 \left(\frac12+2^{-\rho-1}\right)^{q'}
 +\left(1-\left(\frac12+2^{-\rho-1}\right)^{q'}\right)2^{-q}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The diagonal of $Q_\alpha$ is $L_\alpha$.  When $L_\alpha=0$, one product
coordinate of the $\alpha$-sum equals $u^\top Q_\alpha v$ for independent
uniform $u,v$ and is zero with probability
$1/2+2^{-\rho-1}$.  All $q'$ product coordinates vanish with the first term
of \equationref{eq:rc-relation-survival}.  Otherwise the lifted relation is
nonzero, and uniform $A_2$ maps it to zero with probability $2^{-q}$.  If
$L_\alpha\neq0$, the lifted relation is always nonzero, giving $2^{-q}$.
\end{proof}

For example, the four vertices of an affine plane have $\rho=2$, so their
relation survives the nonlinear lift with probability $(5/8)^{q'}$.  The
eight vertices of an affine three-cube have $\rho=0$, and their degree-two
relation survives every seed.  These facts rule out the usual argument that
the rows may simply be treated as independent uniform strings.  They do not,
however, determine the caller-level failure probability: all related points
must first occur in the Ring-LPN batch, enter one partition, and receive
labels with nonzero XOR.

We also account for a local support filter: each party resamples its four
regular supports until their total number of occupied residues modulo 128
is at least 61.  Residues are counted separately in each polynomial, before
coefficient cancellation.  The parties filter independently, before sampling
the shuffle and evaluator seeds.

\begin{proposition}[Ring-LPN batch correctness]\label{prop:rc-ringlpn-correctness}
Assume a uniform oblivious shuffle, uniform Ring-LPN block offsets, and ideal
uniform matrices $A_0,A_1,A_2$.  For the implementation-shaped batch with
$N_{\mathsf R}=2^{20}$, $P=4$, $t=16$, $w=2$, $d=16$, and $q'=q=64$, the
probability that any programmed system is inconsistent is less than
$2.379\cdot10^{-13}<2^{-41.934}$.  If both parties instead apply the local
support filter above, this probability is less than
$4.739\cdot10^{-13}<2^{-40.940}$.
\end{proposition}
The bound counts every possible zero-linear-moment relation by weight and
quadratic rank, then adds the ordinary rank loss in the final compression.
\appendixref{app:revCuckooHash} gives the counting argument.  Filtering changes
each polynomial-pair contribution by at most a factor of $2.044$; the
final-compression bound remains unchanged.  At the former
widths $q'=q=56$, the corresponding bound was only $2^{-37.694}$.  The fixed
eight-bit margin in \equationref{eq:rc-hash-widths} therefore restores more
than 40 bits of batch correctness without repurposing $\lambda$.  The
protocol needs no explicit abort: a skipped inconsistent equation is included
in this correctness error.  This proposition is specific to the Ring-LPN
caller; the AnyField distribution requires a separate analysis.

The dependence on $N_{\mathsf R}$ is material.  At the same evaluator widths,
our present proof gives only $34.213$ bits for $N_{\mathsf R}=2^{18}$ and
$26.218$ bits for $N_{\mathsf R}=2^{16}$.  Deterministic affine-cube relations
dominate both bounds, so increasing $q$ or $q'$ alone cannot improve them.
Accordingly, only the $N_{\mathsf R}=2^{20}$ Ring-LPN row carries the stated
40-bit correctness claim.  The smaller rows in the evaluation are performance
scaling measurements.

\paragraph{Cost.}
Every address appears once in each of the two partition maps, so
$\sum_j|S_j|=2N$.  After setup, local sparse-DPF expansion is therefore $2N$
leaf operations, independent of $t$.  Setup performs one shuffle, two
$d\times q$ binary solves with $\ell$ right-hand-side bits, sparse-set
construction, and sparse-DPF generation.  These are exactly the stages
measured in \sectionref{sec:RevCuckooPerf}.

\subsection{What the descriptor reveals}
\label{sec:revCuckooDistribution}

The placement is hidden, but the public hashes are not distributed like
independent hashes.  The difference has a simple exact description.

Fix a set $A\subseteq[0,N)$ of $t$ distinct addresses.  In the ideal hash
model, let $U$ sample $w$ independent uniform functions
$h_s:[0,N)\rightarrow[0,d)$; the construction above uses $w=2$.  For a sampled
hash tuple $h$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rc-placement-count}
 Z_A(h):=
 \left|\left\{
  \tau:A\hookrightarrow[0,w)\times[0,d) :\
  \tau(x)=(s,b)\Longrightarrow h_s(x)=b
 \right\}\right|.
\end{equation}
Thus $Z_A(h)$ is the number of injective cuckoo placements of $A$ supported by
$h$.  There are $(wd)_t$ injections, and any fixed injection agrees with a
uniform $h$ with probability $d^{-t}$, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rc-expected-placements}
 \mathbb E_{h\gets U}[Z_A(h)]=\frac{(wd)_t}{d^t}.
\end{equation}

The $K=1$ Reverse-Cuckoo experiment chooses a uniform injection $\tau$ first
and then samples a uniform hash tuple consistent with $\tau$.  Summing over
all hidden injections compatible with a fixed output $h$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rc-size-bias-clean}
 \Pr[H=h\mid A]_{\mathsf{RC}_1}
 =\Pr[H=h]_U\,
   \frac{Z_A(h)}{\mathbb E_U[Z_A(H)]}.
\end{equation}
This is the complete leakage channel in the ideal model: Reverse Cuckoo
\emph{size-biases} the ordinary hash distribution by its number of valid
placements.

\equationref{eq:rc-size-bias-clean} also isolates a tempting but incorrect
simulation argument.  Sampling $U$ conditioned on $Z_A(H)>0$ gives equal
weight to every feasible hash tuple.  Reverse Cuckoo instead gives a feasible
tuple weight proportional to $Z_A(h)$.  Hidden placement randomness is already
summed into $Z_A(h)$; it does not remove the multiplicity bias.

\paragraph{The role of dummies.}
The $m-t$ dummies make the real placement a uniform injection into all $m$
bins and provide slack.  They impose no hash equation because $F_s(N)=0$ and
no output because their payload is zero.  Increasing the slack smooths the
placement-count distribution, but it does not change
\equationref{eq:rc-size-bias-clean}: the descriptor is still weighted by
$Z_A(h)$.

\paragraph{Graph intuition for two partitions.}
For $w=2$, represent each address $x\in A$ by an edge from
$h_0(x)$ in the left partition to $h_1(x)$ in the right partition.  A valid
placement orients every edge toward a distinct endpoint.  A tree component
with $e$ edges contributes $e+1$ placements, a unicyclic component contributes
two, and an overfull component contributes zero.  The global value $Z_A(h)$
is the product of these component contributions.  Pair collisions are useful
because they change the component structure and generally reduce $Z_A(h)$,
but the exact channel is a higher-order matching statistic, not a collection
of independently leaked support bits.

\paragraph{Why this is not a generic DMPF.}
For arbitrary chosen inputs, \equationref{eq:rc-size-bias-clean} depends on
$A$.  A party can test public collisions among candidate addresses and
distinguish supports.  Consequently Reverse Cuckoo does not realize the
ordinary DMPF functionality for arbitrary inputs in the standard simulation
model.  It realizes a leakage-augmented functionality that reveals one sample
from the channel in \equationref{eq:rc-size-bias-clean}.  Waterfall Cuckoo
exists precisely to avoid this qualification.

The binary feature evaluator adds algebraic structure beyond the ideal
random-function model.  \namedref{Proposition}{prop:rc-ringlpn-correctness} bounds
the resulting consistency error for the Ring-LPN caller, but it does not turn
the published descriptors into ideal independent hashes.  Our security
assumption below therefore includes the descriptors produced by the concrete
evaluator.  \equationref{eq:rc-size-bias-clean} remains the clean idealized
leakage model used by the attack study.

\subsection{Why sparse Ring-LPN may tolerate the leakage}

In the PCG, $A$ is not an adversarial DMPF input.  It has the form in
\equationref{eq:ringlpn-product-list}.  Each party holds $P=4$ regular
support lists, and every list has one offset in each of $t=16$ blocks.  At the
claimed $N_{\mathsf R}=2^{20}$ point, each block has length
$L=N_{\mathsf R}/t=2^{16}$.  Against a semi-honest corrupt party, that party's
four lists are known and the honest party's $Pt=64$ offsets remain hidden.
Before the public Ring-LPN samples are used, those unknown offsets range over
a space of size $L^{Pt}=2^{1024}$.

Conditioning on a public Reverse-Cuckoo descriptor changes the support prior
by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rc-posterior-reweighting}
 \Pr[A\mid H=h]\ \propto\
 \Pr_{\mathsf{reg}}[A]\,Z_A(h).
\end{equation}
It does not add candidate supports, turn the error dense, or reveal a linear
equation in its positions.  It reweights an already sparse family by a
nonlinear matching count.  Three features make this plausibly benign for LPN:

\begin{enumerate}
 \item \emph{Sparsity is preserved.}  Every posterior candidate still has one
       position in each regular block.  The decoder's basic combinatorial
       obstacle remains finding a tiny support inside a huge ambient ring.
 \item \emph{The leakage is geometric, not linear.}  It describes collisions,
       Hall obstructions, and placement multiplicity among hash images.  A
       linear test of the Ring-LPN syndrome cannot directly consume the
       statement ``this support graph has more matchings.''
 \item \emph{The coefficients remain hidden.}  Reverse Cuckoo depends on
       positions, not on the independent uniform field coefficients.  Even a
       correct partial support hypothesis must still satisfy the large-field
       Ring-LPN equations.
\end{enumerate}

This is intuition, not a reduction.  Sparse decoding is often robust to
partial support information, and stationary syndrome decoding provides a
particularly strong analogy for the failure of linear tests, but neither fact
proves that the matching weights are harmless.  A nonlinear decoder may bias
its search toward high-likelihood supports.  The relevant question is
therefore concrete: does the leakage appreciably reduce the best known
Ring-LPN decoding exponent?

In the stationary setting, the descriptor and support are reused while only
the coefficients change.  Repeated expansions do not reveal fresh independent
placement samples, but an attacker may amortize arbitrary preprocessing of the
single descriptor.  Our assumption permits that preprocessing.

\subsection{Concrete attack evidence for \texorpdfstring{$K=1$}{K=1}}
\label{sec:revCuckooAttacks}

We evaluated the channel primarily at the reduced point
$(P,t,L)=(4,16,4096)$, for which $N_{\mathsf R}=tL=2^{16}$.  This point retains
the implemented batching and Reverse-Cuckoo parameters while making the
leakage-aware experiments tractable.  It is not the full
$N_{\mathsf R}=2^{20}$ correctness point above.  Analytic calculations that
scale to larger rings are identified separately.  All bit counts below are
attack work exponents, and every leakage-aware algorithm is free to ignore the
descriptor.  The experiments use the stronger $K=1$ channel throughout.

\tableref{tab:revCuckooAttackCoverage} separates the attack families we
considered from diagnostics that only describe the channel.  A negative entry
means that the particular attack did not improve the best known exponent; it
does not constitute a generic lower bound.

Exact small-instance experiments do find multi-bit improvements from the
leakage.  This is important: the channel is exploitable, and its effect cannot
be dismissed merely because it is nonlinear.  At production parameters,
however, none of the implemented attacks improves upon the roughly one-bit
gain of leakage-guided regular Prange.  The quadratic formulation and the
stationary cross-expansion analysis identify additional attack surfaces, but
we do not claim to have closed them.

The most important structured attack reduces the ring modulo
$X^{128}+a$.  The expected total number of occupied residues is $60.383$.
This counts selected positions, not nonzero coefficients after folding. The
published conservative statistical-decoding model gives $128.450$ bits at
that mean.  Low folded weights are materially weaker: averaging the same
optimistic model over unconditioned noise gives $122.482$ bits.  We therefore
require the local support filter defined before
\namedref{Proposition}{prop:rc-ringlpn-correctness}.  It accepts each party's sample with
probability $0.501914$ and raises the averaged conservative estimate to
$130.572$ bits. The implementation applies this filter during setup for
the Goldilocks profile $N_{\mathsf R}=2^{20}$, $P=4$, $t=16$.
Each rejection resamples all four supports; later expansions retain the
accepted supports. Other parameter sets retain the unfiltered sampler and
are not certified by this estimate. The rejection is a security requirement,
not a Reverse-Cuckoo mitigation.
The filter tests only selected positions; it does not reject zero
coefficients or prevent cancellation after folding.
\appendixref{app:rc-hash-conditioning} accounts for its effect on programmed-hash
correctness without assuming that the accepted offsets remain independent.

For leakage adaptation after factor reduction, a degree-128 dual word can be
chosen to vanish on any desired set of 127 out of 512 reduced coordinates.
We projected the complete pair posterior into these coordinates and optimized
the resulting global zero-set distribution.  Uniform selection is best for
both expected work and average signal.  A final matching-aware experiment
propagated complete support particles through the reduction; it also found no
gain, but suffered low particle diversity.  Thus the obvious factor attack
does not benefit from the leakage, while a fundamentally better joint
matching-aware decoder remains unexcluded.

The study therefore covers every attack family listed in
\tableref{tab:revCuckooAttackCoverage}, but with different levels of evidence:
some are production cost estimates, some are reduced experiments, and some are
structural formulations or open audits.  It does not prove security against
every decoder, a new structured solver for the quadratic system, a stronger
cross-expansion module attack, or a new algebraic attack on the concrete
feature evaluator.  The scripts, deterministic checks, raw CSVs, and fuller
methodology are recorded in
\path{experiments/rev_cuckoo_lpn_attack_study.md}.

\subsection{Security statement and scope}

The PCG needs a distinguishing assumption, not merely hardness of recovering
a sparse support. We state it for the joint sequence of samples and the
concrete published descriptors.

Fix a polynomial count $P\geq2$, block count $t\mid N_{\mathsf R}$,
and a field $\mathbb F_q$. Define
\[
 \mathbb R:=\mathbb F_q[X]/(X^{N_{\mathsf R}}+1).
\]
Fix a corrupted party $p\in\{0,1\}$, a number of expansions $Q$, and
a total adversarial work budget $T$. The experiment
$\mathsf{RC\text{-}LPN}_b$ has challenge bit $b\in\{0,1\}$.

\begin{enumerate}
 \item Independently sample the parties' regular supports, applying the
 local support filter stated before
 \namedref{Proposition}{prop:rc-ringlpn-correctness}.
 Retain these supports for all $Q$ expansions.
 \item Run the concrete $K=1$ descriptor-generation procedure on every
 product list in \equationref{eq:ringlpn-product-list}, with fresh
 shuffle and evaluator randomness. Let $\mathsf H$ contain all public
 evaluator seeds and programmed descriptors.
 Give $\mathsf H$ and party $p$'s supports to the adversary.
 \item For each expansion $u\in[0,Q)$, sample independent coefficients
 from $\mathbb F_q$ on both parties' supports, including zero. Write $e^{(u)}_{v,a}$ for
 party $v$'s polynomial $a\in[0,P)$. Give party $p$'s coefficients
 to the adversary.
 \item For each expansion $u$, put $r_0^{(u)}:=1$ and sample
 $r_i^{(u)}\gets\mathbb R$ independently for $i\in[1,P)$.
 Write $\mathbf r^{(u)}:=(r_i^{(u)})_{i\in[0,P)}$.
 If $b=0$, set
 \[
 Z^{(u)}:=\sum_{i\in[0,P)}r_i^{(u)}e^{(u)}_{1-p,i}.
 \]
 If $b=1$, sample $Z^{(u)}\gets\mathbb R$ independently.
 Give the adversary every pair $(\mathbf r^{(u)},Z^{(u)})$.
 \item The adversary outputs a bit. Its work, including preprocessing of
 $\mathsf H$ before receiving the samples, is at most $T$.
\end{enumerate}

The supports, filter, descriptors, and corrupted-party information have the
same distribution in both experiments. Only the honest party's noisy ring
samples are replaced; in the random experiment, those replacements are
independent of the hidden supports and descriptors.

The coefficient law matches the implementation; no zero rejection is required.
For comparison with nonzero-coefficient models, couple the samplers until
the first zero coefficient. Across both parties and $Q$ expansions, the
statistical distance is at most $2PtQ/q$, approximately $Q\,2^{-57}$ for
the Goldilocks profile with $P=4$ and $t=16$.
This bounds the change in any fixed attack's success probability, not its
work exponent, and does not establish a hardness ordering between the laws.
The reported nonzero-coefficient attack estimates remain comparisons under
that model; the assumption below uses uniform field coefficients.

\begin{assumption}[Leakage-robust decisional Ring-LPN]
\label{ass:rc-decisional-lpn}
For the chosen parameter family and polynomially bounded $T,Q$, the advantage
\[
 \epsilon_{\mathsf{RC}}(T,Q):=
 \sup_{\mathcal A:\operatorname{time}(\mathcal A)\leq T}
 \left|\Pr[\mathcal A(\mathsf{RC\text{-}LPN}_0)=1]
       -\Pr[\mathcal A(\mathsf{RC\text{-}LPN}_1)=1]\right|
\]
is negligible in the computational security parameter.
The concrete point studied here has
\[
 (N_{\mathsf R},P,t,d,q',q_{\mathsf{hash}})
 =(2^{20},4,16,16,64,64),
\]
with the Goldilocks coefficient field $q=2^{64}-2^{32}+1$.
Here $q_{\mathsf{hash}}$ denotes
the feature-compression width called $q$ earlier in this section.
\end{assumption}

At fixed concrete parameters, $\epsilon_{\mathsf{RC}}(T,Q)$ is a conjectured
advantage bound, not a consequence of the attack work estimates.
Support-recovery experiments provide evidence about some attacks on this
assumption; they do not establish decisional security.

This is the additional pseudorandomness assumption needed to expose the
Reverse-Cuckoo descriptors in the Ring-LPN application.
A full PCG security proof must also compose the distributed generation
subprotocols and reusable DPF realization with the target correlation
functionality. The separate hash-correctness bound applies to the filtered
caller distribution.
